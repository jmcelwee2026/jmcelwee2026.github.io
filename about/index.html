Professional identity · Skill areas · How to reach me

I am a Juris Doctor candidate at Ave Maria School of Law focused on applied legal technology — using AI-directed research, government-data investigation, and workflow automation to produce accurate, reviewable legal work product across regulatory and public-sector settings.

Across every setting, applied technology has been the constant: AI-directed federal regulatory and funding research, multi-model AI workflows for commercial real-estate research, contract-management platform evaluation for public-sector legal operations, and desktop process automation within a state trial court. Each is developed as a case study in Selected Work.

Credentials

The practice of law is being handed a genuinely new horizon, and in proactive effort to read the water — and not get lost at sea — I have been charting it on purpose.

Most of that has meant AI and the law: Clio's Legal AI track end to end — Fundamentals, Emerging, and Advanced (December 2025 through August 2026); Harvey's Legal Engineer certification at the foundational level (2026); and, as a law student before licensure, attending two Rossdale continuing-legal-education webinars on generative AI in practice — Mastering Artificial Intelligence & ChatGPT Law in 2026 and Cutting-Edge AI, ChatGPT and Generative AI Law in 2026.

Beneath the new instruments sit the enduring ones: LexisNexis Applied Legal Research certificates across secondary sources, statutory law, case law, and litigation — and one cheerful outlier, a Negotiation Professional certificate, because however far the horizon moves, the work still comes down to people in a room.
